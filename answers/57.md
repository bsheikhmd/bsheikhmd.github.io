*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Several things can cause it. New pain in a knee that had been doing well for years is worth having checked.

The most common cause found in knees that need a second operation years later is loosening, where the implant parts come loose from the bone. In a large series of repeat knee operations, infection was the leading cause in the first two years and loosening the leading cause after that. The plastic spacer between the metal parts also wears slowly, and heavy use or extra weight may speed this up. Some spacers have worn out early because of how a particular product was made or packaged. Parts that were not well aligned, or a knee not well balanced during surgery, are also linked to failure.

Infection can start years later too, when bacteria from the teeth, bladder or skin travel through the blood to the knee. Other causes include a knee that has become unstable, a break in the bone next to the implant, irritated tendons around the kneecap, and pain coming from the hip or lower back.

Surgeons usually start with an exam and X-rays. When infection is possible, blood tests for inflammation come next, and fluid may be drawn from the knee for testing.

In general:

- Fever, chills, redness, swelling, or pain at rest as well as with activity need prompt attention.
- Tell your dentist and other doctors that you have a knee replacement.
- Note when the pain started and what makes it worse, and bring that to the visit.

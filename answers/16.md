*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Yes. A past PRP injection does not rule out a knee replacement. As with other knee injections, the question is timing.

No study has looked at PRP on its own. The best evidence comes from a large insurance database study that grouped PRP with other injections that contain no cortisone, and even with simply drawing fluid from the knee. Together, these were linked to more infection in the new joint when given within three months of surgery, and the risk was highest within the first month. Studies of knee injections in general point the same way. In one, the higher risk lasted up to about six months.

The authors suggested that the needle itself, not only what is injected, may play a part. That idea has not been proven.

The evidence has limits. It comes from records, not trials, so it shows a link rather than proof of cause.

In general:

- Tell your surgeon about any PRP or other injection in that knee, and when it was given.
- If surgery is already scheduled, ask before getting another injection in that knee.
- The longer the gap between an injection and surgery, the lower the risk appears to be.

*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Often nothing. A knee replacement that looks unusual on an X-ray can work well and stay comfortable for years.

What matters more than how it looks is whether it is stable. A stable knee that bends, straightens and holds you up is doing its job, whatever the picture suggests.

That does not mean every kind of imperfect placement is the same. Some show themselves early, with pain, stiffness or a knee that does not track right. Others cause no trouble for years and then show up as loosening. Which is why a knee that looks unusual gets followed rather than ignored, even when it feels fine.

So the short answer is that how it looks on the day does not tell you much, and how it holds up over ten or fifteen years might. That is watched over time, not decided from one X-ray.

In general:

- Feeling well is the more important signal, not the picture.
- An unusual looking replacement is not automatically one to redo.
- Keep the follow-up X-rays so changes get noticed early.
- New pain, giving way or swelling is worth reporting, whatever the film showed before.

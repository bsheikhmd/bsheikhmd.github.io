*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Most twisted ankles in children are sprains. A true growth plate fracture is less common than many parents are told.

The growth plate is a layer of cartilage near the end of a growing bone. It is weaker than the ligaments around it, so doctors have long worried about it. Children with pain on the outer ankle and a normal X-ray have often been treated as if they had a growth plate fracture.

MRI studies have changed that view. In a study of 135 children ages 5 to 12 with pain on the outer ankle and a normal X-ray, MRI found a growth plate fracture in only 4. Most had a sprain. Some also had a small chip of bone pulled off by a ligament that the X-ray did not show. Children with and without a fracture recovered equally well in a removable brace.

Not every twisted ankle needs an X-ray. Doctors use bedside checklists, such as the Ottawa Ankle Rules, based on where the ankle is tender and whether the child can walk. In a review of 12 studies of more than 3,000 children older than 5, the Ottawa rules picked up 98.5% of fractures and cut X-rays by about a quarter. A similar rule, tested in 2,151 children, missed no important fractures.

Low-risk injuries are usually treated simply. In a trial of 104 children, those given a removable ankle brace had better function at 4 weeks than those in a walking cast, and more were back to their usual activities. The evidence comes from a few small trials.

Some growth plate fractures are more serious. These usually involve the shinbone at the ankle, not the thin outer bone. They may need a cast, or surgery if the pieces have shifted. In hospital studies of these fractures, between 13% and 27% of children later had the growth plate close too early. Some then needed surgery for a short or crooked leg. The risk was higher in younger children and when a gap remained between the pieces.

Growth plate fractures happen about twice as often in boys, because girls finish growing earlier.

In general:

- If your child cannot stand or walk, or the ankle looks deformed, have it checked promptly.
- Ask whether an X-ray is needed. For many twisted ankles it is not.
- After a growth plate fracture of the shinbone, ask about follow-up visits to check growth.
- A return to sport is usually reasonable once pain and tenderness are gone.

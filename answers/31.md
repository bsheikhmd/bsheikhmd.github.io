*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Probably, for most people, but no study has settled how soon.

Surgery and long flights each raise the risk of blood clots in the legs, which can travel to the lungs. The question is whether flying adds to that risk after surgery.

The few studies are small. In the largest, people who flew home within days of a hip or knee replacement had no more clots than people who did not fly. A study of people who took long flights before surgery found the same. But a study from Scotland found more clots in people who took short flights to and from the hospital, though it counted only a handful of clots. Clots were uncommon in every group, so none of these studies can rule out a small difference.

In the general population, the longer the flight, the higher the clot risk. For ordinary travelers on flights over four hours, compression stockings greatly reduce clots that cause no symptoms. That has not been studied after a knee replacement.

No guideline sets a waiting time. Surgeons' advice ranges from two weeks to six months, and some airlines and travel insurers ask for a doctor's note.

In general:

- Ask your surgeon before booking a flight, especially in the first weeks.
- A long flight is a bigger question than a short one.
- Ask whether you need extra clot protection for the trip.
- Pain, swelling or tenderness in the calf after a flight needs to be seen promptly.
- Sudden shortness of breath or chest pain needs emergency care.

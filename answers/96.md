*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

It depends on the signs. Worry when the limp comes with a fever, when the child will not stand or walk on the leg, or when the child seems ill. These can be signs of an infection in a joint or a bone. The child should be seen by a doctor the same day, because delayed treatment can damage the joint and the growing bone.

The most common cause is a short-lived swelling inside the hip joint that is not an infection (transient synovitis). Other causes include a bruise, a splinter or something stuck in the foot, and a small crack in the shinbone (toddler's fracture).

A toddler's fracture often does not show on the first X-ray. It heals well, with a short time in a cast or splint, or sometimes with no support at all.

If a limp does not get better, or comes with other signs that worry you, it could be something more serious. Talk to your child's doctor.

In general:

- Get your child seen the same day for a limp with fever, refusal to stand or walk, or a child who seems ill.
- See your child's doctor for a limp that does not get better.
- If the first X-ray is normal and the limp continues, ask whether it could still be a small fracture.

*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Usually not.

For years, many people with a joint replacement were told to take antibiotics before seeing the dentist, in case bacteria from the mouth reached the new joint. That advice has changed. The current orthopedic guideline says routine antibiotics before dental work may not lower the risk of a later infection in the joint. Dental guidance says the same.

The studies behind this are large but based on records, and the evidence is rated low quality. Across them, dental work did not raise the risk of infection in the new joint, and antibiotics taken beforehand did not lower it. Antibiotics carry their own risks, including side effects and resistant bacteria.

Research is thinnest for people with a weakened immune system, a previous joint infection or a redo replacement. The one large study that included redo knee replacements found antibiotics did not lower infection. For more invasive dental work in people with a severely weakened immune system, expert panels are split.

In general:

- Routine checkups and cleanings do not call for antibiotics.
- Daily care of the teeth and gums matters.
- If you have a weakened immune system, other serious health conditions, a previous joint infection, or a redo replacement, it is worth discussing this further with your surgeon and your dentist.
- If your surgeon and dentist give different advice, ask them to settle it together.

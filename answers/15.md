*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Yes. A past gel injection does not rule out a knee replacement. As with cortisone, the question is timing.

Gel injections, also called hyaluronic acid or viscosupplementation, go into the same joint that may later be replaced. Studies from large insurance databases found that a gel shot given shortly before surgery was linked to a higher rate of infection in the new joint. How short that window is differs between studies: within one month in some, within two or three months in others. Beyond three months, no added risk has been found.

When studies compared gel and cortisone directly, neither shot carried clearly more risk than the other.

The evidence has limits. It comes from records, not trials, so it shows a link rather than proof of cause. A large study of Medicare patients found no link at all, even for shots within four months of surgery.

In general:

- Tell your surgeon about any gel shot in that knee, and when it was given.
- If surgery is already scheduled, ask before getting another gel shot.
- Several gel shots given well before surgery have not been linked to more infection.

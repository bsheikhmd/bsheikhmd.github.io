*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Almost certainly not. Clicking, popping and grinding after a knee replacement are common. A large share of patients hear something from their new knee, and plenty of people who never had surgery hear noises from their own knees too. Some knees are simply louder than others, and that alone does not mean anything is wrong.

The noise usually comes from the metal and plastic parts moving against each other, or from the kneecap riding over the front of the implant. Some implant designs are noisier than others, so two people with equally good results can sound very different.

On its own, clicking is rarely how a failing knee shows itself. When a replacement is loosening or wearing out, the warning signs are pain and swelling, not noise. People who hear their knee do tend to be a little less happy with it, which is worth knowing going in.

One exception has a name: patellar clunk. A band of scar tissue near the kneecap catches on the implant and causes a painful clunk. It is a separate problem that needs to be evaluated, and it can be treated.

In general:

- Clicking by itself is not a sign that your knee is loose.
- Clicking with new pain, swelling or giving way needs to be looked at.
- A painful clunk from the kneecap is worth reporting.
- Keep your follow-up visits even if the noise does not bother you.

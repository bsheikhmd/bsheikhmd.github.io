*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Nobody knows for certain, and there is no evidence that it means anything is wrong with the implant. Plenty of people with arthritis say their joints feel the weather, and some still do after a knee replacement.

One small study asked knee replacement patients about this directly. Surgery took the weather-related aching away in about half of those who had it beforehand. Others kept it, and some noticed it for the first time after surgery. It was more common in women and in people whose other joints also ached with the weather, which suggests it has as much to do with the person as with the knee. Their knees still worked as well as everyone else's.

Whether the weather really changes pain is less clear. Across studies of arthritis, cold and damp are the conditions most often linked to more pain. But studies that matched pain flares, or pain after joint replacement, against local weather records found no link. How weather would act on a joint has not been worked out.

In general:

- Aching with the weather is not unusual after a knee replacement and is not a warning sign on its own.
- Aching together with warmth, swelling, redness or fever is not the weather and needs to be seen by your surgeon promptly.
- Pain that keeps getting worse, whatever the weather, is worth reporting.

*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Probably not. A continuous passive motion machine, or CPM, is a device that slowly bends and straightens your knee for you while you lie in bed. For years it was standard after a knee replacement.

The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons now lists it among the things to avoid using routinely after a knee replacement, and physical therapy guidelines say the same. Pooled results from twenty trials found the machine improved knee bending by two or three degrees, too small to matter to anyone actually using the knee, with no gain in function or pain and no shorter hospital stay. One trial found the group without a machine went home sooner.

Used instead of therapy rather than on top of it, it looks a little different. One older trial compared a home machine directly against professional therapy and found no difference at six months in bending, knee scores or stiffness, at lower cost. But those patients had spent days in hospital first. No study has looked at a machine in someone who goes home the same day, which is now common, so nobody can say what it adds there.

The machine is not a shortcut. What it substitutes for is your own effort.

In general:

- Guidelines advise against routine use after an uncomplicated knee replacement.
- Bending the knee yourself does more than a machine bending it for you.
- No study has tested one in patients who go home the same day.
- A knee that stays stiff in the first weeks is worth reporting early.

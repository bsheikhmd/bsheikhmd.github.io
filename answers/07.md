*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

For ordinary recreational running, no. The largest study of this compared runners with people who did not run, and the runners had less hip and knee arthritis than the people sitting still. That is worth saying plainly, because it is the opposite of what most people believe.

Competitive running is a different story. Elite and professional runners, and people running high mileage for many years, have more arthritis than either group. So the pattern is not a straight line where more is worse. The worst places to be are running at an elite level, and not moving at all.

Two honest limits. This is what goes together, not what causes what. The people who run tend to be lighter and have fewer prior injuries, and those things protect a knee on their own. And a knee that has already been injured, particularly a torn ligament or cartilage, does not follow the same rules.

In general:

- Recreational running does not appear to wear out a healthy knee.
- Sitting still is associated with more arthritis than running is.
- High mileage over many years is where the risk goes up.
- A knee with a prior serious injury is a different question.

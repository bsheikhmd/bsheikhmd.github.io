*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Most of the bend comes back early. In one study, nearly all of it had returned by six weeks on average. Smaller gains can continue for up to a year.

How far your knee bends after surgery depends a lot on how far it bent before. In a large study, the bend before surgery was the strongest predictor of the bend after. A knee that was stiff going in often ends up bending less.

Research often calls a knee stiff when it bends less than about 90 degrees three months after surgery. That is not a strict line, because it depends on where the knee started. For comparison, one study of healthy older adults found that stairs and getting up from a chair take roughly 90 to 120 degrees.

If the knee stays stiff despite therapy, your doctor should look for a cause. The usual next step is a manipulation: the surgeon bends the knee while you are under anesthesia. It is best done within the first three months.

Knees that still feel stiff a year after surgery are linked to worse function and less satisfaction.

In general:

- Most of the bend comes back in the first six weeks.
- How far it bends depends partly on how far it bent before surgery.
- If you are falling behind, tell your surgeon early rather than waiting.
- If a manipulation is needed, it is best done within the first three months.

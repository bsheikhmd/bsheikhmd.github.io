*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Usually yes. Most rounded backs in teenagers come from slouching, not from a problem with the spine.

This kind of rounding is smooth, and it goes away when the teen stands up straight. According to the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons, it is rarely painful, does not get worse, and usually causes no problems in adult life. Exercises that strengthen the back can help.

Slouching on a couch, in bed or over a phone rounds the back while the teen is doing it, but it has not been shown to cause a lasting hunched back. Doing homework at a desk, holding a screen at eye level and getting up to move during long periods of sitting make sense, although there is little research behind them. Slouching has not been clearly shown to cause back or neck pain. No good studies show that store-bought posture braces or straps help.

Less often, the rounding comes from the bones. Some bones in the upper back grow wedge-shaped (Scheuermann's kyphosis). The curve is stiff and does not straighten when the teen stands up. An X-ray confirms it. Most teens need only checkups and exercises; a brace is used for some larger curves, and surgery is rare.

In general:

- Encourage your teen to take breaks from long sitting and screen time, and to stay active.
- If your teen cannot straighten the back when standing tall, ask your child's doctor to check it.
- See a doctor sooner for back pain that does not go away, a curve that seems to be getting bigger, or numbness or weakness in the legs.

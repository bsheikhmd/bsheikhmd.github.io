Not necessarily. The picture and the symptoms often do not match. Some knees that look awful on film belong to people walking around fine, and some that look only moderate belong to people who can barely get through a day.

Across large groups, worse X-rays do go with more pain on average. For any one person, the film predicts much less. Some of that is how differently people register pain, which is measurable and not imagination.

So the X-ray is not what decides it. What decides it is what the knee is costing you. Not whether you can still play sports at seventy, but whether you can get through the grocery store without turning back at the second aisle, whether it wakes you at night, whether you have stopped doing things you would otherwise do.

The guidelines take the same view, and specifically leave out people with little pain or disability no matter how bad the film looks. Replacing a knee that looks terrible but is not bothering you much tends to disappoint.

In general:

- A bad looking X-ray on its own is not a reason to operate.
- Ask what the knee is stopping you from doing, not what the film shows.
- Pain that wakes you at night counts more than pain you can walk off.
- Waiting is a real option when you are still doing what you want to do.

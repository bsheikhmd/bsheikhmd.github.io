*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Not in the ways that matter most to patients, as far as studies show so far.

In a robotic knee replacement, the surgeon still does the operation. A robotic arm or guidance system helps plan and make the bone cuts. It reliably places the parts closer to the plan, with fewer knees far off target.

That precision has not turned into a better knee. In a large trial where neither the patients nor the people assessing them knew which operation was done, there was no meaningful difference in how the knee felt a year later. A trial that followed patients for more than ten years found no difference in function, loosening or the need for a redo. Pooled studies agree. The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons guideline reaches the same conclusion for the short term.

Large record studies found a slightly shorter hospital stay and fewer problems during that stay with the robot. Trials have not shown a clear difference in safety. National joint registries have found no fewer redo operations so far, though most follow patients for only a few years.

Whether some knees, such as badly deformed ones, gain more from a robot has not been tested.

In general:

- For most patients, robotic and standard knee replacements give similar results.
- More precise placement has not yet been shown to make a knee last longer.
- Ask your surgeon which method they use most often.

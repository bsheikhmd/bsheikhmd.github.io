*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Sometimes, but not always. Feeling unsteady is common after a knee replacement. It can come from weak thigh muscles or from ligaments around the new knee that are too loose.

In a Dutch study of more than 900 people, about 1 in 5 still felt their knee was unstable a year after surgery. Most of them had felt unstable before surgery too. People who felt unstable had more pain and more trouble with daily activities.

In the first days, weak thigh muscles are a common reason. Some nerve blocks used for pain relief weaken these muscles for a time, and in two studies the knee buckled more often with one type of block than another.

True instability means the ligaments around the new knee are too loose, either in some positions or all of them. It is one of the most common reasons a knee replacement is redone, often within the first few years. When the knee is loose while bent, people often describe swelling that keeps coming back, feeling unsteady going down stairs, and aching around the knee. That description comes from expert reviews, and there is no agreed test for instability.

How the bone is cut can matter. If too much bone is taken from the back of the thigh bone, or the top of the shin bone is cut sloping too far backward, the knee can end up looser when bent than when straight. Setting the joint higher or lower than where it was before surgery can also change how tight the ligaments are through the bend. In knees redone for instability, correcting these problems restored stability. This comes mostly from redo studies and expert reviews, and X-rays alone do not reliably show which knees are unstable.

Strengthening and bracing are often tried first, but they have not been tested well. When the knee is truly unstable, a redo operation usually helps. People redone for instability improve less than people redone for other reasons, and more often feel their expectations were not met. Changing only the plastic insert fails more often than replacing the metal parts too.

In general:

- Unsteadiness in the first weeks usually improves as the thigh muscles recover.
- Swelling that keeps coming back, or a knee that gives way on stairs, is worth reporting.
- A knee that gives way with new pain, warmth or swelling needs to be seen promptly.

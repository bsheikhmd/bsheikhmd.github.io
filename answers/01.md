*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

When doctors say exercise, they mean movement done on purpose, often enough that your body notices. Not a gym. Not sweating or straining or a plan you dread. Walking, standing up from a chair without using your arms, going up stairs.

There is a difference between activity and exercise. Walking around the house is activity. Walking a route that gets a little longer over time is exercise. That difference is most of what doctors mean by the word.

For a stiff hip or knee the point is to keep the muscles around the joint strong and the joint moving. Movement does about as much for the pain as the usual pills do. Low-impact movement does not appear to wear the joint out faster. There is no single best kind. The best kind is the one you will keep doing.

Hurting during movement is common with arthritis and does not automatically mean you should stop.

Things that count, none of which require a gym:

- Get up and move a little every hour rather than sitting through the day.
- Park at the far end of the lot and walk the extra distance.
- Stand up from a chair a few times without using your arms.
- Walk the length of the house or the driveway when you think of it.
- Move in a pool if being on your feet hurts.

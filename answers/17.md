*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Nobody knows exactly why, but night aching after a knee replacement is common early on and usually fades.

The one study that tracked this directly followed people from before surgery to a year afterward. Night pain in the knee was common beforehand and still common in the first weeks after surgery, then became uncommon by one year. People who still had night pain tended to have more pain overall and more nerve-type pain, burning or tingling rather than aching.

Poor sleep was a separate story. About half the people in that study slept poorly before surgery, and about half still did a year later, even as their night knee pain faded. The knee is not always the reason for a bad night.

This rests on one small study, so it is a reasonable picture rather than a settled one. What matters most is the direction. Night pain that is slowly easing is part of recovery. Night pain that is getting worse, or comes with swelling, warmth, redness or fever, is different.

In general:

- Aching at night in the first months is common and usually improves.
- Night pain that is getting worse, not better, is worth reporting.
- Pain with swelling, warmth, redness or fever needs to be seen promptly.

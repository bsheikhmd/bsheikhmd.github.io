*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

For carefully chosen patients, usually yes.

Large studies that matched same-day patients with similar patients who stayed in the hospital found no more complications or readmissions in the same-day group. Pooled studies agree, and same-day surgery costs less.

Who goes home matters. Readmission is more likely with older age, heart failure, chronic lung disease, high blood pressure or higher body weight, and in people who need help getting around before surgery. In one study, people with some of these risks were readmitted less often when they stayed one night. Yet a large US health-system study found same-day discharge safe even for sicker patients within an established program.

Not every study agrees. A national study from England found more readmissions after same-day knee replacement, and more people needing a second procedure within a year to loosen a stiff knee. The risk was highest in each hospital's first few same-day cases.

At freestanding surgery centers, emergency transfer to a hospital was rare in one large series. Every transfer was for a medical problem, such as fainting, heart rhythm, blood pressure or breathing, not the knee itself.

Nearly all of this evidence comes from records, not trials.

In general:

- Whether to go home the same day depends on overall health and is decided with your surgeon.
- Heart or lung disease is a reason to ask whether one night in the hospital is safer.
- At a surgery center, ask which hospital it transfers patients to if something goes wrong, and how that care is billed under your insurance.
- A knee that stays stiff in the first weeks is worth reporting early.

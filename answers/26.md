*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

For most people, a very long time. Large national joint registries show that about 9 in 10 knee replacements are still in place 15 to 20 years after surgery, and about 8 in 10 at 25 years. No knee replacement lasts forever, but many people never need a second operation.

Age at surgery matters most. The younger you are when you have it, the more likely you are to need a redo someday. For people over 70 at surgery, the lifetime chance is about 1 in 20. That figure comes from a study that combined hips and knees. For men in their late 40s, it is about 1 in 4. Among younger patients, men are more likely than women to need a redo. People in poorer general health are more likely to need one at every age.

The most common reasons for a redo are loosening of the parts, an unstable knee, infection, and pain without a clear cause.

These numbers describe large groups. They cannot tell you what your own knee will do.

In general:

- Most knee replacements last 20 years or more.
- The younger you are at surgery, the more likely you are to need a redo someday.
- If you are younger, weigh this with your surgeon when deciding when to have surgery.

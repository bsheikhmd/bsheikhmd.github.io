*Last reviewed: September 29, 2026*

Usually within a few weeks, and often sooner after a left hip than a right one. Timing varies from person to person.

Most studies test how quickly people can press the brake in a driving simulator. After a right hip replacement, braking was back to its pre-surgery level within two to six weeks, depending on the study. After a left hip, braking was barely affected, since the right foot does the braking. The surgical approach made little difference.

In surveys, most people were driving again within about six weeks. Women, people with more pain or stiffness, and people who had the right hip replaced took longer. In one study of about a thousand people after a hip or knee replacement, few reported a car accident in the first three months, and none was hurt. No study has compared crash rates with people who did not have surgery, or looked at getting in and out of the car.

According to the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons, driving is usually safe once you are off opioid pain medicine and your strength and reflexes are closer to normal.

In general:

- Do not drive while taking opioid pain pills.
- Ask your surgeon when to start, especially after a right hip replacement.
